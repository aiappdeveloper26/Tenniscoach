Build a lesson plan

Choose a focus and duration; the plan below updates. The instructions are written as a court-side checklist: what you set up, what you say, what players do, and what to watch for.

Coach notes & reflection

Week-by-week curriculum

A flexible progression map, not a rigid syllabus. Repeat a week when the group needs more practice; move on when most players show the success indicators safely.

How to run each lesson

  1. State one goal. Tell players what they are trying to achieve in simple, observable words.
  2. Demonstrate once or twice. Show the whole action at normal speed, then highlight one key cue.
  3. Start easy. Use self-drop, hand feed, slower ball, shorter distance or larger target.
  4. Keep everyone active. Use stations, pairs, multiple targets and short turns; avoid long queues.
  5. Observe outcomes. Look for balanced movement, controlled contact, appropriate decisions and enjoyment.
  6. Adjust immediately. If success is low, simplify one variable. If success is high, add movement or a choice.
  7. Finish with transfer. End with a game that uses the skill, then ask each player what helped.

Safety, inclusion & coaching standards

Stop immediately for pain, dizziness, heat-illness signs, lightning, unsafe court conditions or equipment failure. Follow venue emergency procedures and local safeguarding requirements.

Age 8–12 coaching reminders

  • Use concise instructions and demonstrations, then get them moving quickly.
  • Rotate activities every few minutes if attention or engagement drops.
  • Teach technique through simple cues and outcomes; avoid overloading with technical detail.
  • Develop both sides, both directions and a range of movement patterns.
  • Keep competition low-stakes and cooperative; celebrate improvement and effort.
  • For most beginners, modified balls and reduced court dimensions support longer rallies and better decisions.
  • Do not prescribe maximal lifting or exhaustive conditioning; prioritise movement quality, coordination, recovery and enjoyment.
